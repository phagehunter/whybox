whybox

A closed room with a locked machine in it. You can put something in and read a number off the display. You cannot open the machine. Your job is to work out what is inside.

That is a stripped-down version of experimental science, and it turns out to be a good way to find out whether someone, or something, can actually do science rather than just score well at it. Those two look identical from the outside.

Play a round below. It takes about ten minutes. Most people reach the top score long before they can explain it, which is the entire point, felt rather than read.

What you just played

One sandbox out of an open-ended family. A sandbox has three parts: specimens, the things you can submit; an assay, the measurement; and an oracle, the sealed machine that runs it and hands back a small report. Hiding the oracle is what forces you to infer rather than look up.

Oracles come in three temperatures. A wet one runs a real experiment: real, slow, expensive, and nobody knows the right answer. A damp one runs a computer model trained on real measurements: fast, and wrong in whatever ways the model is wrong. A dry one, like this, runs a rule somebody invented: no biology in it at all, so nothing you find here is a discovery about nature. In exchange, the answer is known exactly, which is the only way your claims can be graded rather than argued about.

Two things to know before trusting any number here

In a browser, nothing is hidden. This page runs on your own machine, so the rule is readable in developer tools by anyone who thinks to look. That is a property of browsers, not an oversight. What is protected instead is the part that matters for an experiment: the commitment fingerprint means the rule cannot change while you play. The Python version runs the rule in a separate process, which is better, and still not a security boundary.

The understanding score is word matching. See the warning above. It is a rough instrument, published with its own weakness measured.

The full toolkit

This page is one world with one investigator: you. The Python package runs the same thing with a fixed number of rounds, a lab notebook, baselines that optimize without understanding, and a grader that keeps the two numbers apart on purpose. It has no dependencies, which is deliberate: there is no environment to debug in week one.

git clone https://github.com/phagehunter/whybox.git
cd whybox
python3 -m whybox run codon-lite --agent human --rounds 15

Write your own sandbox, and the only real requirement is a system where you know the rule and the investigator does not. The guide is short. The interesting sandboxes are the ones where a capable investigator fails in a way you can explain.
